Step 02 — Discovery

We remove every unknown before a single quote is written.

Discovery is methodical. We map the environment, the occasion, the scale, and the decision process — not because we need to fill a form, but because every one of these variables has a direct bearing on what your piece should be and what it should cost. Surprises at this stage cost nothing. Surprises after production begins cost everything.

What we map

Four variables that define the scope of every commission

⬡

Environment

Where will the piece live? Ambient light, display angles, surface material, room scale — all of these change how depth and detail behave inside optical glass. We understand the setting before we design for it.

◎

Purpose & Occasion

Is this a once-in-a-generation award, an annual recognition, a corporate gift, or a commemorative edition? Purpose defines the emotional register. Occasion defines the timeline. Both define what the piece must achieve.

⊞

Scale & Logistics

One centrepiece or five hundred individual gifts? Dispatched to a single venue or shipped internationally? Scale and logistics shape production planning, quality controls, and the form the final pieces take.

◷

Decision Timeline

Who is involved in approval, and when do they need to see what? Understanding the decision structure from the outset means the prototype and approval process is built around your organisation — not ours.

Why there is no estimate here

Every Eyekonika commission is different. A single collector piece for a private individual and a run of three hundred corporate awards for a global institution are not variants of the same product — they require entirely different approaches to design, production planning, and quality verification. Providing a number before Discovery is complete would mean providing a number that is not accurate. We do not do that. Once Discovery concludes, the quote we produce reflects exactly what your piece requires — and nothing it does not.
